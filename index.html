three extracted trajectories

Real-world robotic navigation models lean on simulated data because good, well-formatted real data is sparse. Bristle is an automated pipeline that turns real estate walkthrough videos into training data for embodied AI navigation: short clips of continuous camera motion, each with a camera trajectory, depth maps and a destination on the floor.

pipeline

  1. Segment. PySceneDetect splits each house tour video from RealEstate10K into clips at scene cuts.
  2. Reconstruct. MegaSaM recovers metric depth maps and camera poses for each clip.
  3. Find transitions. Cuts and fades left inside a clip are detected, so no trajectory crosses one.
  4. Extract trajectories. A greedy search takes the farthest destination that stays in view and unoccluded for the whole path, then locates the floor point beneath it.
  5. Filter. QwenVL looks at each result and rejects drone shots, stairs, people and the like.
  6. Measure. Path length, displacement, speed and depth statistics are computed per trajectory.
